# Campus → home church

**The decision:** start with the small university fellowships, because the students carry the app home
to their churches.

**Agreed.** It is the only loop in this whole file that repeats by itself: a fellowship of forty
students is forty doors into forty churches, and each of those churches has young people who go to
other campuses. Nothing else in the plan has that shape.

But it only pays if the loop is built properly, because "the students will take it to their home
churches" is where most founders lose the year. It is true, and it converts almost nothing, unless
three things are true: the intro is specific, the demo lives on the student's own phone, and the
leads are **grouped before they are called**.

---

## 1. What the fellowship actually is

Not a customer. **A distributor.**

| The fellowship gives you | You give the fellowship |
|---|---|
| 30–60 students from 30–60 different home churches | The roll, free or at 9,000 |
| A room where the thing visibly works | Proof it works, in front of their friends |
| 40 warm introductions a term | A story they can tell at home in December |

Price is secondary. 9,000 is a filter, not revenue — it separates the fellowships that will actually
use the roll from the ones that will nod and forget.

---

## 2. The intro must be one exact action, not a sentiment

**Wrong:** *"Tell your pastor about this."* → the student forgets by Sunday, or says "there's this app"
in the car park, and nothing happens.

**Right:** one action, done in front of you, in ninety seconds:

> "Open WhatsApp. Send your pastor this: *'Pastor, there's a thing we use at fellowship for attendance
> and follow-up. My friend [name] built it — can he call you this week?'* Then send me his number."

Three things that makes true:
1. **The student sends it, not you.** A pastor opens a message from his own young person.
2. **It asks for a call, not a purchase.** No price reaches the pastor through the student.
3. **You leave with a number, not a hope.** No number, no intro — be strict about this.

**Never** give a student the price list, a flyer, or a target. The moment a student is selling, the
pastor stops listening and starts protecting.

---

## 3. The demo has to live on the student's phone

The whole loop dies at one point: the student goes home, and the pastor asks *"show me."*

So before a student leaves the room, they must be able to open the thing themselves and show a real
screen — their fellowship's roll, this term's attendance, who came back. Not a PDF, not a video, not
your laptop. **Their phone, their fellowship's data.**

That is a product requirement, and it is small: a student-level view that a member can open and show.
It is also the single highest-leverage thing you could build in December — higher than any feature for
pastors, because it is the only part of the product that travels without you.

---

## 4. Group the leads before you call a single one

This is the part everyone skips, and it is where the money is.

Forty students do not mean forty customers. They mean **forty churches, clustered** — and the cluster
is the sale:

- A student from a small rural church: that church may genuinely not have 17,000, and chasing it alone
  is a wasted fare.
- **Five students from the same district or denomination:** that is one call to one district overseer,
  and he can carry twenty churches at 17,000 each — the same "body above" logic as TJM and Manifest.

So keep a sheet with three columns: **student · home church · district/denomination.** When a district
reaches three or four names, stop collecting and call the district. The campus network is not a list
of churches — it is a **map of where the churches are concentrated**, and the map is the asset.

| What you collect | What it becomes |
|---|---|
| 450 home churches across 15 fellowships | too many to call, worth little |
| 450 names grouped into ~40 districts | 40 calls, each worth 3–20 branches |

---

## 5. The calendar — this is November work, not December work

Students travel home in December. **The intros must be collected before they travel**, because a
student who has already gone home is a phone number in another district and no longer a warm door.

| When | What |
|---|---|
| **This week (Nov 17–21)** | The one question in the fellowships. Not a pitch — the roll question. Two visits maximum; TJM and Worship House still carry Friday |
| **Late Nov – early Dec** | The ninety-second intro, done in the room, on the student's phone. Collect names + districts |
| **December (students home)** | The student shows the app at home. You call the churches and districts they opened |
| **January** | The districts that came back, priced per branch. This is where December's work turns into money |

So the honest reading: **this lane does not help Friday.** It builds January. That is fine — but only
if it costs you two visits this week and not five.

---

## 6. Close the loop so it repeats

Two questions, asked at the moment a church activates:

1. **To the church:** *"Which schools do your young people go to?"* → the next fellowship to visit.
2. **To the fellowship, a month later:** *"Three of your members' churches are now on it — here is
   what they are seeing. Who else is from a church that needs this?"*

That second message is the whole engine. It is proof, it is a reason for the fellowship to keep
sending names, and it costs nothing to send.

---

## 7. What not to do

- **No commission, ever.** Not a shilling per church. It turns a friend into a hustler, it makes the
  price negotiable, and it will be the first thing a pastor distrusts.
- **No price in a student's mouth.** They carry the intro; you carry the number.
- **No "tell your pastor about it."** One message, one number, in front of you.
- **No touring fifteen fellowships this week.** Two visits. The phone calls carry Friday.
- **No counting campus money as Friday money.** Fifteen fellowships is 135,000 — a quarter of one TJM
  conversation. Its value is the map, not the cash.
