# "Is 17,000 a lot for a university fellowship?"

**Yes.** For a room of students it is roughly a week of food, and asking for it in that room does
three bad things at once: it makes the room a customer when it should be a reference, it teaches
students the price before they have any money, and it puts a student leader in the position of
collecting from his friends.

But the fix is **not a discount.** It is the buyer.

> **17,000 is a BRANCH price, not a student price. A fellowship is rarely the branch — the church
> behind it is.**

---

## 1. The 30-second test

Ask, in this order, and stop at the first honest yes:

| Question | If yes → |
|---|---|
| **1. Does a mother church sit behind this fellowship?** (Grace Pentecostal Nansana behind Expanse; Christ's Heart Mukono behind Koinonia; The Worship House behind Kiwatule) | The **church** pays 17,000. The fellowship is free. |
| **2. Is there a ministry/denomination above it that wants reports?** (Phanerro's centres, TJM's branches, a chaplaincy, a mission board) | **They** pay, per branch. Never the fellowship. |
| **3. Does the fellowship itself have money** — termly dues, alumni, a conference, a patron who funds it? | The **executive committee** pays, and it comes out of the fellowship's budget, not the students' pockets. |
| **4. None of the above?** | **Free.** It is a proof site and a contact list, not a customer. Walk away from the money without embarrassment. |

**The student executive is not the same buyer as a student.** A fellowship with a treasurer, a budget
line and a patron is an institution. A fellowship that passes a basket on Friday is a room. Price the
institution, never the room.

---

## 2. Why "free for fellowships" is a strategy, not a discount

You are not giving away a product. You are buying three things with it, and they are worth more than
17,000:

1. **Proof you can point at.** "It runs at [fellowship], ask them." That sentence is what converts the
   mother church, and it is what converts TJM's other 31 branches.
2. **Thirty warm contacts a week** — the roll is literally a list of young people who are already
   churchgoing, in a city where every pastor wants exactly that list.
3. **The language.** Two weeks inside a fellowship teaches you how they actually talk about
   attendance. You cannot buy that.

And the hard truth: **a free fellowship converts to a paying church about ten times more often than a
half-price fellowship converts to anything.** Discounting gets you neither the money nor the proof.

---

## 3. The leak you must pre-empt

The moment one fellowship is free, the question comes:

> *"So can our church be free too?"*

Answer, in one sentence, without apologising:

> "The fellowship is free because it is where I prove the thing works. The church is where it gets
> paid for. If your church wants it, it is 17,000 per branch — and I will set it up myself so nobody
> has to do the work."

Never say *"free for now."* **"Free for now"** teaches them to wait. **"Free for fellowships, paid by
churches"** is a rule, and rules do not expire.

---

## 4. What this does to the Friday number

Be honest with yourself: the plan's **136,000** worship-layer line only exists if a **mother church**
pays it. If those eight rooms turn out to have no church behind them, that lane is worth **zero cash**
and a great deal of pipeline.

| | UGX |
|---|---|
| TJM (32 branches) | 544,000 |
| The Worship House (4 sites) | 68,000 |
| Worship layer **if** mother churches pay | 136,000 |
| **Floor if they don't** | **612,000** |

So: TJM and The Worship House carry Friday. The fellowships are how you get next month — and how you
get the sentence you will use in every December call.

## 5. The case that actually gets asked: a university fellowship with **no** mother church

A hall fellowship, a campus Christian union cell, forty students in a lecture room, a treasurer, a
basket on Friday, and nobody above them. This is the hardest version of the question, and 17,000 is
**not** a lot — *if* it comes from the fellowship's own budget instead of from students' pockets.

Four routes, in this order. Stop at the first one that works.

### Route 1 — The fellowship's own budget (usually the answer)

Do not compare 17,000 to a student's pocket money. Compare it to **one Friday collection**.

> "What do you collect on a Friday? … So this is one Friday. Once. Not per term, not per member, not
> every time somebody new joins. You pay it once and it runs for as long as the fellowship exists."

Let **them** say the number out loud. A fellowship that raises 40,000 on a Friday has just told
itself that 17,000 is cheap; you did not have to argue it. And if the arithmetic genuinely doesn't
work — 400 shillings a head, twenty people — then this is Route 4, and you should say so first,
before they have to.

**Who you ask:** the treasurer and the chair, never the room. Never take money that students had to
put in personally out of their own pockets — that is the line where a tool becomes a burden and
where your name travels badly across a campus.

### Route 2 — The chaplaincy (the "body above" that most people forget)

A fellowship with no mother church almost always still sits under an office: the **Dean of Students**,
or the university **chaplaincy** — St. Francis Chapel on the Anglican side at Makerere, the Catholic
chaplaincy, the chaplaincy offices at Kyambogo, MUBS, UCU and the rest.

That office has three things a students' fellowship does not: **a budget**, **a reporting duty**, and
**oversight of several fellowships at once**.

> "Who signs off on the fellowships' records at this university — the chaplaincy or the Dean of
> Students?"

One chaplaincy is not one customer. It is **every fellowship on that campus** — which is the same
multi-site shape as the Prayer Waves and TJM's branches. Ask for one campus, price the fellowships
under it, and you have a network without a denomination.

### Route 3 — The alumni (the real asset nobody counts)

A fellowship with no church behind it has something better: **people who came through it.** In Uganda
a campus fellowship is the seedbed of churches — its alumni are now youth leaders, associates,
pastors, and founders. That list is the actual sale.

> "Who came through this fellowship and is pastoring somewhere now? Three names is all I need."

This is the single highest-value question you can ask in a students' room, and it costs the fellowship
nothing to answer. It converts a free site into three warm introductions, and those three men have
budgets.

### Route 4 — Free, and say it before they ask

> "For fellowships this is free — forever. You are where I prove it works. If your church, your
> chaplaincy or your patron ever wants it for a branch, that is 17,000 — and I set it up myself."

Say it first, in those words, and the room relaxes, because nobody has to work out whether they can
afford you. Then the whole conversation becomes the roll: who came, who came back, who is in the
discipleship track, who is serving.

---

## 6. The one-line summary

- Fellowship **with** a mother church → the church pays 17,000. Fellowship free.
- Fellowship **under a chaplaincy** → pitch the chaplaincy, one office, many fellowships.
- Fellowship **with its own budget** → one Friday's collection, once, paid by the executive.
- Fellowship **with nothing** → free, and leave with three alumni names.

**The room is never the customer. Sometimes it is not even the door — sometimes it is the address
book.**

## 7. Decision (17 Nov 2026): the campus rate is **9,500**, applied as an introductory rate

There is **no tier in the code** — that was built and then removed, because it was not what was asked
for. What exists instead:

| | |
|---|---|
| **Church price** | **17,000** (`ACTIVATION_STANDARD_FEE_UGX`, unchanged) |
| **Campus price** | **9,500**, reached by setting `ACTIVATION_FEE_UGX=9500` for the window |
| **The screen** | says *"Introductory rate: UGX 9,500, one time. This is a launch rate — the standard rate applies once it ends."* It no longer says "test pricing", and no longer prints 17,000 |

### The one thing to be clear-eyed about

The override is **global**. While it is set, every church that activates pays 9,500 as well. So the
campus rate is a *price cut for the whole deployment for the length of the window*, and the window has
no expiry of its own — clearing it is a manual delete plus a redeploy.

Keep the window as short as the campus conversations require, and clear it the hour it ends.

## 9. The env var: `ACTIVATION_FEE_UGX` (what to literally type)

One variable, server-side, read once at boot in `lib/activation/index.ts`.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Name** | `ACTIVATION_FEE_UGX` |
| **Should be** | empty / deleted, for normal trading |
| **Format** | whole UGX digits only — `1000`, `9000`, `17000` |
| **Range** | `100` – `10000000` |
| **Not allowed** | `"1,000"` · `1000.5` · `-500` · `+500` · `1e3` · `UGX1000` · `NaN` · `0` |
| **Empty or invalid** | falls back to the standard **17,000**, and logs an error — a bad value can never become the price |
| **Valid and ≠ 17,000** | that amount, plus `ACTIVATION_FEE_IS_TEST_PRICING = true`, a loud warning in the logs, and a test-pricing banner on the activation screen |
| **Applies to** | **every** church, every tier. There is no per-tier env var |
| **Takes effect** | only after a redeploy — it is read at module load, not per request |
| **Where** | Vercel → Settings → Environment Variables. Set it the same in **Production and Preview** |

### What that means in practice

- **Selling this week: leave it empty.** Churches pay 17,000, the app records 17,000, and the green
  campus panel stays off the screen — it only renders when the fee differs from 17,000.
- **Do not put 9,500 in it.** It would charge *every church* 9,500 and label your live selling as
  test pricing — and the screen prints the campus line instead of a warning, so it will not read as a
  test, but the scope is still every church.
- **The one legitimate use:** a `1000` end-to-end test before you take a real payment — set it,
  redeploy, activate a test church with your own phone, confirm the prompt, the webhook, the ledger
  row, `activation_status = active` and the money landing. Then **delete it and redeploy.**
- **Why Production and Preview must match:** the webhook compares the amount Na'jiki settled against
  the amount the *initiating deployment* recorded. Two environments with different fees leave a
  church that has paid permanently unactivated.

### The related variable

`ACTIVATION_PROMPT_WINDOW_MS` — how long the screen tells a pastor they have to approve the prompt on
the handset. Default **10 minutes** (600000 ms); the copy is generated from the value, so the screen cannot
disagree with the setting. Same rules: whole milliseconds, invalid values fall back to the default.

## 10. "What if I put 9,500 in the env var until Monday 9pm?"

Don't. Three things happen, and the second one is fatal.

**1. Every church pays 9,500** — it is one global number, not a fellowship switch.

**2. The activation screen shows every church a green panel reading, verbatim:**

> *"**Campus tier** — UGX 9,500, one time. Not per term, not per member."*

The panel renders whenever the override is active (`isTestPricing` in
`components/activation/ActivationClient.tsx:428`), so a **church branch** — a 17,000 customer — is told
on screen that it is on the campus tier at the campus price. It no longer says "test", which is worse,
not better: there is no warning left, only a tier and a price that are both wrong for that buyer. And
it spreads — the first pastor who screenshots it sets your church price at 9,500 for the district.

**3. It lands on the exact days you are closing.** Friday to Monday is the whole close window:

| Close | at 17,000 | at 9,500 | Lost |
|---|---|---|---|
| TJM, 32 branches | 544,000 | 304,000 | **−240,000** |
| The Worship House, 4 sites | 68,000 | 38,000 | −30,000 |
| 15 fellowships | 135,000 | 142,500 | +7,500 |

The fellowships gain **7,500**. TJM alone loses **240,000**. That is the whole trade.

**4. There is no expiry.** Monday 9pm only happens if you remember to delete it *and* redeploy. Forget,
and every church from Tuesday onward is also 9,500 — quietly, forever, with a warning in a log nobody
reads.

**5. The ledger keeps it.** A church that pays 9,500 has a permanent `activation_payments` row at
9,500. Two prices now live in your own books, and the lower one is the one that travels.

**If a fellowship wants to pay before the tier is wired:** take the name and the collection date,
leave them on the free fellowship arrangement, and invoice 9,500 the day the tier lands. Do not lower
the world to reach one room.
