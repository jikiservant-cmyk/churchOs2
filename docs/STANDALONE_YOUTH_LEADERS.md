# Standalone Youth Leaders — the hunting system

No PAG. No COPE. No umbrella, no bishop, no committee. One person, one church, one decision,
17,000 shillings. This is how you find them, what you say, and what you give them.

**One warning up front:** there is no registry of independent youth leaders. Nobody publishes a list.
So this document is not a list — it is a **hunting system**: seven places they gather, the filter for
who is worth a message, the three-message script, and the daily quota that makes the numbers work.

---

## The four kinds of standalone youth leader, ranked by how fast they pay

| # | Type | Does he control money? | Speed to 17,000 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Young church planter** — a 22–35 year old who planted his own church | ✅ He *is* the pastor | **Same day.** Best target on this page |
| 2 | **Independent youth ministry founder** — runs his own youth ministry/NGO, not under a bishop | ⚠️ Partly — he can influence several pastors | 1–2 weeks |
| 3 | **Campus CU leader** — elected annually, belongs to a church back home | ❌ No money, but he brings a church home | 2–4 weeks |
| 4 | **Youth leader inside one church** — the youth pastor/leader of a single congregation | ❌ The senior pastor pays | 1–3 weeks, needs the pastor in the room |

**Chase #1 first, hard.** A young church planter is the only standalone who can pay you in the
conversation. He is also the easiest to reach: he is on Instagram, he answers DMs, and nobody
gatekeeps him.

## The seven hunting grounds

### 1. Campus Christian Unions — the highest-density pool in Kampala
Every university CU has a committee of 8–15 elected leaders, and every one of them belongs to a
church. One CU visit = 10 warm youth leaders in a room.

- **Makerere University Christian Union (MukCU)** — confirmed contact **+256 777 345 125**,
  St Francis, Makerere · mukchristianunion.org
- Then work down the campus list: **Kyambogo, MUBS (Nakawa), KIU, Ndejje, Nkumba, Uganda Christian
  University (Mukono), Bugema, Victoria University, Kampala University, Mutesa I Royal, St Lawrence,
  ISBAT, UMI**.
- How to get in: attend their *fellowship* (usually Wednesday or Friday evening), ask for the
  "CU chairperson or the discipleship coordinator" by name, and offer a 20-minute slot at their
  next executive meeting. Not a sermon slot — an *executive* slot.
- Ask each committee member the one question that turns a student into a lead: **"Which church do you
  come from, and who leads the youth there?"** That one question maps a whole campus to churches.

### 2. Young church planters near campuses
Church plants cluster where students are. These are named, verified examples of exactly the profile:
- **Trinity Chapel Kampala** — Isaac & Rahab Macharia, planted as a young church near universities,
  now independent, worship-music driven, youth-focused (calvaryweb.net story).
- **Grace Church Kampala** — Pius Muzingu, planted within walking distance of four major
  universities (gracechurch.org/kampala-church).
- **Road to Redemption Ministries** — Namuwongo; founded by Ps. Sandra Baingana, a young pastor with a
  large TikTok following. Independent, self-founded, youth-heavy.
- **New Wine Church Kampala** — Ps. Kristine Wright, plant meeting at Seb Hotel, Najjanankumbi
  (Entebbe Rd), +256 78 420 24 40.
- **Christ's Church Uganda** — founded by Steven Sebyala from home fellowships; youth-oriented,
  multiple congregations under Fred Mugambwa.

**Your own search string to find 30 more:** search Facebook and Instagram for
`"church" + "Kampala" + "plant"` or `"ministries" + "Nansana" / "Kira" / "Makindye" / "Mukono"` and
look for pages with **under 5,000 followers, posting weekly, with a named young pastor**. Church
plants market themselves constantly because they're recruiting — that's exactly why you can find
them.

### 3. TikTok, Instagram and YouTube — the Christian creator layer
Uganda's Christian content space is full of young preachers, worship leaders and "weird pastor"
style creators with 10k–300k followers and no institutional structure at all.
- Named examples of the type: **Weird Pastor (Wisdom Mwongyera)** — one of Uganda's biggest TikTok
  creators, from a pastor's home; **Ps. Sandra Baingana** — TikTok/Instagram-visible pastor.
- Search: `#UgandaGospel`, `#KampalaChurch`, `#YouthForChrist`, `#Balokole`, `#UgandaTikTok` +
  Christian keywords. Filter to accounts posting **weekly, in Luganda or English, with a ministry name
  in the bio**.
- These people sell for commission. A creator with 50k followers who posts one video about "the system
  my church uses" is worth more than ten cold calls — but only if the commission is clear and the
  product is real.
- **Do not pay upfront for posts.** Pay per activated church, same day, exactly like everyone else.

### 4. Independent youth camps and conferences
Camps are where youth leaders from 30 churches sit in one field for a week. Most are run by
standalone ministries, not denominations:
- **Gold Youth Camps** (Vast Grace Missions) — targeting 1,000 teens across Uganda in 2026,
  registration already open · vastgrace.org/gold-youth-camps
- **CTMI Kampala Youth Camp** — 3–5 September 2026 (calendar item, not this week)
- **Harvest Christian Ministries** (Pr Fred & Grace Muyimba) — Thursday youth service plus an annual
  youth conference · harvestchristianministries.org
- Approach: offer to run a **free 45-minute "church admin" workshop** at the camp. You leave with the
  phone numbers of every youth leader in the room. Cost to you: one afternoon.

### 5. Bible colleges — youth leaders in training
Every Bible college in Kampala has 30–200 students who are already leading youth somewhere on
weekends: Kampala Evangelical School of Theology (Makerere Hill), Glad Tidings Bible College
(Makerere Hill Rd, P.O. Box 2560), and the PAG/Full Gospel training institutions.
One 30-minute class visit per college beats a week of DMs. They are broke, but their *churches* pay.

### 6. Social-media youth groups and WhatsApp/Telegram communities
Kampala youth ministries run large WhatsApp and Telegram groups (prayer groups, "youth altar",
campus connect groups). The **admins** are standalone leaders with reach. Find them by joining
publicly-posted group links from church Instagram pages.
**Never spam a group.** DM the admin, one message, offer the free account, ask for a 10-minute call.

### 7. Secondary-school Christian clubs
Scripture Union reports ~3,970 schools with clubs. The **patron teachers** and student leaders are
standalone, and they funnel into local churches by design. Slower, but a school-club leader who
becomes a youth leader at 22 is a customer for a decade.

### Which one to use, and for what

- **UYIN / IRCU is the direct answer to your question.** One room containing youth leaders from
  Catholic, Anglican, Orthodox, Adventist, Muslim and Pentecostal churches that otherwise share
  nothing. If you want a single meeting that produces leaders from churches with no relationship to
  each other, this is it.
- **AEE is the most commercially usable.** Interdenominational, church-partnership by design, 55 years
  in Uganda, and they run youth programmes — they will happily introduce a tool to their partner
  churches.
- **SU, FOCUS and YFC are pipelines**, not rooms: each one continuously produces leaders who graduate
  into churches. Get your 30-minute module into their training cycle and it repeats every intake.
- **UJN and UJCC are credibility**, not volume. Being able to say "we work with the Jubilee Network"
  opens doors that a cold DM never will.

### The catch, stated plainly

These are **donor-funded networks, not buyers.** They will want a partnership, an MOU, or a free
licence for their members — and none of them will pay 17,000 per church. That's fine: you want their
**rooms and their lists**, not their money. The churches in the room still pay individually.

Pitch them a **free licence for their member churches** and ask for one thing in return: **the list, and
a slot at their next gathering.** That is the whole trade.

## BORN AGAIN ONLY — the networks you actually meant

The inter-faith layer (UYIN/IRCU) mixes Catholics, Anglicans, Orthodox, Adventists and Muslims. If you
want **born-again churches only**, that's a different set of bodies — and one of them says so in its own
words.

**Their own website, verbatim:** *"NFBPC is **not ecumenical or interfaith** in belief or practice, and is

That sentence is exactly your requirement: born-again only, and **non-denominational** — meaning its member

## The filter — is this one worth a message?

Score each prospect out of 5. **Only work the 3s and above.**

| Question | Points |
|---|---|
| Does he lead or plant an actual church (not just a ministry brand)? | +2 |
| Is his church 100+ people, or youth 30+? | +1 |
| Has he posted in the last 14 days? (alive and reachable) | +1 |
| Is he inside Kampala / Wakiso / Mukono? (you can physically turn up) | +1 |
| **Total** | **/5** |

A 5/5 is a same-day visit. A 2/5 gets a DM and nothing more. Never spend an hour on a 2.

## The three-message script (individuals, not bishops)

**Message 1 — the DM/WhatsApp (send 20 a day, 3 lines max)**
> "Bro/Sis, I'm a Ugandan founder — I built a church system that keeps members, tracks attendance and
> sends SMS to youth at 50/= each. I'm giving it free to 30 youth leaders this month in exchange for
> honest feedback. Are you the one who handles youth records at your church?"

**Message 2 — the voice note (only if he replies)**
Send a 40-second voice note, not text. Your voice is the trust. Say: who you are, that you're a
student-founder, one sentence on what it does, and *"give me 10 minutes on a call and I'll show you on
my screen."*

**Message 3 — the meeting ask (never sell in DM)**
> "When are you at church? I'll come to you — I can do Tuesday evening or Wednesday after 4."

**Then in person:** laptop open, 300-name CSV imported live, an SMS sent to his own phone, usher
passkey demo. Then, and only then, the 17,000.

## What a standalone leader gets (different offer from a bishop)

| Bishop gets | Youth leader gets |
|---|---|
| Diocese dashboard | **"Founding Youth Leader"** title + certificate |
| 1,000 free SMS | Free account for his youth ministry once the pastor approves |
| Volume discount | **3,000/= commission** per activated church through his code |
| A meeting in his office | A WhatsApp group with the other 29 founding leaders |
| Invoice | Paid the **same day**, every time |

Status matters more than money to this group — give both, and pay instantly. The speed of your
payment *is* your reputation. A youth leader who is paid within an hour tells ten friends; one who
waits a week tells nobody and stops.

## The daily quota (this is the whole engine)

| Activity | Per day | Conversion |
|---|---|---|
| DMs sent | 20 | ~25% reply = 5 conversations |
| Voice notes sent | 5 | ~40% agree to meet = 2 meetings |
| Meetings held | 2 | ~50% activate = 1 church |
| **Churches per week** | **5–7** | and each one refers 1–2 more leaders |

At that rate: **~25 churches a month, alone, with no bishop.** Two months of discipline and the
89-activation problem disappears permanently. Add three ambassadors doing the same and it's 75/month.

## What fits in *this* week

Be clear-eyed: standalone youth leaders are a **2–6 week compounding channel**, not a Friday cash fix.
The only standalone sub-segment that pays this week:

1. **Young church planters near universities.** They decide and pay themselves. Target the cluster
   around Makerere, Kyambogo and MUBS, and the two verified plants above.
2. **MukCU and one or two other campus CUs** — a single executive-meeting visit yields 10 leads, of

Everything else — the creator layer, camps, Bible colleges, school clubs — start it this week, harvest
it next month. That's not a consolation prize: it's the difference between needing 89 activations once
and never needing them again.

## Risks, stated plainly

1. **Age and credibility.** You will hear "you're a student, what do you know?" Answer with the app on
   screen, not with words. Then let the pastor's dashboard do the talking.
2. **Bypassing the pastor.** The single fastest way to get banned. The youth leader *always* introduces
   you upward, and the commission is paid from your margin — say that out loud to the pastor.
3. **Creator economics.** Influencers will ask for money upfront. Pay per activation or not at all.
4. **Churn.** CU leaders graduate, youth leaders move churches. Recruit twice what you need.
5. **The 3,000 is public.** Print it on the ambassador card. A commission that looks hidden becomes a
   scandal; one that's on paper is just business.
