## 2. What a "manifest centre" actually is

It is not a branch in the denominational sense. It is a **livestream congregation**: a group of people, a
screen, a room — usually 20 to 100 people — led by a **centre leader** who is not a paid pastor. They gather
for the Thursday and Sunday broadcasts "from Kasese to Canada."

**That is both the opportunity and the trap:**

- **Opportunity:** a centre leader behaves exactly like the buyer you have been hunting — young, decides
  alone, no procurement, personally responsible for a group of people.
- **Trap:** the centre is under **ministry oversight**. A centre leader can adopt you and then be told to
  stop. Nothing at centre level is truly independent.

## 3c. Is the campus arm controlled by the ministry, or does it just happen?

**Short answer: it doesn't "just happen" — but the people running it are volunteers, not staff, and they have
no money.** Both facts change how you sell.

### The chain of command, as far as it is publicly visible

```
Phaneroo Ministries International (Apostle Grace Lubega)
        │
Manifest Fellowship — the outreach arm, national level
        │
Regional Coordinators  (e.g. "Manifest Central", "Manifest East Campuses")
        │
Campus fellowship leaders  (students — Makerere, Kyambogo, KIU, St Lawrence…)
        │
The room you sat in on Tuesday night
```

**Evidence it is centrally structured, not organic:** a document posted online purporting to be minutes of a
**Manifest Central Leaders meeting (8 August 2020)** lists leaders from various Manifest fellowships, an
address from the **Regional Coordinator**, guidance on **"filling out report forms,"** and **financial
contributions for ongoing Manifest programmes.** Treat the document as unverified, but the shape it describes
matches everything else visible: numbered weekly meetings, regional sub-accounts (Manifest East Campuses,
Manifest Fort Portal, Manifest Kenya, Manifest Tz), and ministry pastors sent to preach at campuses.

### Who is paid

| Role | Paid? | What they can decide |
|---|---|---|
| Ministry staff (a small team — third-party estimates put Phaneroo headcount around 30) | Yes | Policy, branding, budget |
| Regional Coordinators | Likely ministry-appointed, small or no stipend | Which campuses run, who leads them, what gets reported |
| **Campus fellowship leaders (students)** | **No — volunteers** | Meeting content, invite list, which free tools they use |
| The room | No | Nothing |

**And note the direction of money: it flows *up*, not down.** If the 2020 minutes are accurate, campus groups
are asked to *contribute to* Manifest programmes. A campus fellowship is therefore not a buyer — it is a
**reporting unit with a collection plate**.

### What that means for you — the important part

1. **Never talk price to a campus leader.** They have no budget and no authority; a price tag only makes you
   the man who wants money from students.
2. **The campus leader is a *proof site*, not a customer.** Free account, live in ten minutes, and a working
   attendance record they can show their coordinator.
3. **The buyer, if there is one, is the Regional Coordinator** — the person who collects report forms from
   dozens of campuses. Their pain is *administrative*: they chase numbers by WhatsApp and spreadsheet.
   That is a real, identifiable buyer with a small budget.
4. **The actual money in Phaneroo is the manifest centres**, not the campuses — those are led by adults who
   run their own rooms and can pay 17,000 without asking anyone.

### The general rule you should carry into every ministry you meet

> **The pain sits at the bottom. The money sits at the top. They are almost never the same person.**

So the deal is always two steps: **prove it at the bottom, get the decision at the top.** Makerere proof →
Central campuses coordinator. Oikos leader proof → TJM founder. Branch pastor proof → COPE secretariat.
That is the same move every time, and it is the only one that survives contact with how these organisations
actually work.

## 4. What you are actually selling Phaneroo — and what you are not

**Do not pitch "capture your converts."** They already have a **New Converts Registration** form on their
website. If you walk in saying they can't capture converts, you look like you didn't do your homework.

**Pitch these three instead:**

1. **Centre attendance and health.** 670 centres, and the ministry's view of them is WhatsApp messages from
   volunteers. One passkey per centre, any phone — and the coordinator finally sees which centres are alive,
   which are drifting, and which have stopped meeting altogether.
2. **Follow-up of the converts the website already catches.** A form produces a spreadsheet. A pipeline
   assigns each new convert to the **nearest centre** and sends three SMS. That is the gap between their form
   and a disciple.
3. **Campus fellowship lists.** 200+ institutions, each group with a membership nobody can produce on paper
   when a leader graduates.

## 5. The honest warnings — three of them

**1. They already have a vendor and an app.**
Their app was built by an outside studio (a23labs). Expect *"we'll build it ourselves"* in the first meeting.
Have the answer ready: *"You built the front door — streaming, devotionals, giving. This is the back office
for 670 centres. You could build it in a year, or switch it on for one centre on Thursday."*

**2. The conflict is real but narrower than people say — here is exactly who and what.**

I overstated this the first time. Phaneroo is **not** in documented conflict with "all those organisations." It
breaks down into four different things, and only one of them affects your week:

| Relationship | What is actually documented | Risk to you |
|---|---|---|
| **Campus CUs / FOCUS** | The 2022 *Making Phaneroo Manifest* report documents disputes inside Christian Unions at **Makerere, MUBS, UCU and St Luke Ntinda**, serious enough to reach university administration | 🔴 **High** — this is the room you are selling into this week |
| **Individual senior clerics** | **Bishop Zac Niringiye** publicly called Phaneroo's Shs200bn project *"robbery in Jesus' name"* (2025); a public clash with **Pastor Tom Mugerwa** (Mutundwe) over Kololo grounds | 🟡 Only matters if those specific people are your gatekeepers |
| **Mainline church teaching** | Phaneroo's own 2017 statement says it faces anti-Phaneroo circulars, conferences, books, and is *"a taught topic in the confirmation classes of children as young as eight"* — i.e. some mainline catechism teaches against it | 🟢 Low — you are not selling to Catholic or Anglican dioceses |
| **NFBPC / COPE / born-again bodies** | **No documented institutional conflict that I can find.** Both describe themselves as broad and non-denominational, and many Phaneroo attendees also belong to born-again churches | 🟢 **Low** |
| **Public opinion** | Loan, mega-church and Naguru land controversies (2025–26). The ministry denies wrongdoing and has issued statements | 🟡 Medium — it colours how a cautious pastor reads your logo |

**So the practical rule is not "avoid Phaneroo."** It is **read the room**:

- **Pitching FOCUS, CU leaders or a university chaplaincy?** Don't lead with Phaneroo. Use TJM Makerere
  instead. Same proof, no baggage.
- **Pitching a born-again bishop, a COPE church or a mid-size network?** Phaneroo is neutral-to-positive —
  most of them respect the youth numbers even where they dispute the doctrine.
- **Serving Phaneroo centres?** Do it quietly and well. You are not required to pick a side, and the product
  is theologically neutral by design.

**And this is the same "who else do you work with?" test you will face everywhere.** Have one sentence ready:
*"I work with any church that wants its records straight — CU fellowships, born-again networks, campus
movements. I don't take sides in disputes; I keep names and attendance."*

**3. Centralised control cuts both ways.**
The upside is one yes unlocks hundreds of centres. The downside is that one no, or one change of policy,
shuts down every centre you set up. Do not build your month on it.
