# Three products, one week

**The question this answers:** he also has a **school attendance system** and a **SACCO system**.
Do they change the plan?

**The short answer:** not before Friday. But one of them is arguably a better business than churchOs,
and it is already sitting on the same mechanic.

---

## 1. The mistake to avoid this week

Three products, four days, one of you = three pitches each getting a third of the follow-up. The
churchOs stack is already computed: TJM 544,000 + Worship House 68,000 + the fellowship layer
136,000 = **748,000 floor**, with Phaneroo as the upside. That stack does not survive being split.

Worse, a second product does not just halve your time. It gives a hesitant buyer somewhere to send
you: *"ah, but you also do schools — come back when you've decided what you are."*

**So: zero new outreach for the other two before Friday.** Not one call, not one flyer.

---

## 2. What the three actually are

| Product | Who decides | Cycle | This week? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **churchOs** — church roll, attendance, report forms | one pastor, or one network coordinator | days, if warm | **Yes — this is the week** |
| **SACCO system** — member register, savings, loans | a board/committee, but a chairman drives it | weeks — but they have real money | No. December/January pipeline |
| **School attendance** — learners, staff, term records | one director/proprietor | weeks, and **wrong season right now** | No |

### Why not schools *this* week, specifically

Term III runs **14 September – 4 December 2026**, and the next term opens **2 February 2027**. Right
now every head teacher in the country is in exams and end-of-year marking, and every proprietor is
chasing fees. November is the worst month in the year to ask a school to sit down and look at a
system. Their buying window is **the December holiday and the first weeks of Term I**.

That is not a reason to drop schools. It is a reason to put them in **December**, when you have the
time and they have the quiet.

### Why SACCOs are more interesting than they look

- **Scale:** registered SACCOs went from ~5,798 in 2015 to **over 31,800 by March 2025**, and Bank of
  Uganda figures in 2026 put it above **33,000**. Only ~11% of Ugandans use formal financial
  institutions — the SACCO is the bank for everyone else.
- **Money:** they hold members' savings and lend. They have budgets, and they already spend on
  stationery and accounting.
- **The apex exists:** **UCSCU** (Uganda Cooperative Savings and Credit Union, est. 1972) is the
  national apex, owned by its members, with ~**1,385 SACCOs** under it — and it already sells
  branded stationery to SACCOs. Phone **+256 41 4233601**. That is a denomination-shaped door.

The catch: a SACCO board is a committee, and committees do not say yes in four days. So it is a
December–January lane, not a Friday lane.

## 3. The thing you have actually built

Look at the three side by side and the same shape appears:

> **A group with a roll, records kept in a book, and a body above it that wants a report.**

- Church: members, attendance book, the district/denomination report
- SACCO: members, savings and loan book, the union/apex return
- School: learners, class register, the proprietor's or district's return

And the mechanic you already built for churchOs is the reusable part — **one invite code activates one
branch, and every branch sets itself up.** That is exactly what a 32-branch church, a 3-campus school
group and a 1,385-SACCO apex all need, and none of them can afford to have you install it 32 times.

**So the honest positioning is not three products.** It is one mechanic — *network activation, no
installation* — applied to three kinds of organisation that keep their records in books. Say that
sentence to any of them and it still lands:

> "One dashboard instead of the book. Every branch activates itself with one code."

That sentence is your IP. The logos on the front are not.

## 4. What to do about the other two, cheaply, this week

Three moves, none of them sales calls, total time under an hour:

1. **If either product already has a user** — even one — get from them, today, two things: **one
   sentence of proof** ("this is what changed for us") and **one referral** to a neighbouring school
   or SACCO. That is 10 minutes and it is worth more than any new outreach you could do.
2. **Write the apex down**: UCSCU, **+256 41 4233601**. Not this week — but a single introduction
   there in January is worth more than thirty cold SACCO calls.
3. **Do not mention any of this to a church buyer.** A pastor who hears you also do SACCOs will
   wonder which one you are actually committed to. One product per conversation, always.

## 5. The order after Friday

| When | What |
|---|---|
| **Now – 4 December** | churchOs only. TJM, Worship House, the fellowship layer, the PK network, Phaneroo when the name comes |
| **December (schools closed, 5 Dec – 31 Jan)** | **Schools.** Head teachers are quiet, proprietors are planning next year, and a system demo in December is a Term I sale. Target the **chains** — one proprietor, several campuses — because that is the multi-site shape your code already does |
| **January (before AGMs)** | **SACCOs.** Start from the apex, then a district union, then individual SACCOs. Their pain peaks when they compute dividends and file returns — **ask each one when their AGM is**, because that date is your deadline frame |

The school calendar and the AGM cycle give you two natural deadlines. Use them the way 17,000 uses
December: *"this price is for the term that starts in February."*

## 6. The one question I still need answered

**Which of the two already works?** Live users, even one, changes everything — because a product with
one paying customer is a December business, and a product with none is a January distraction.

And the follow-up: **do you already know a school proprietor or a SACCO chairman personally?** If
yes, that is another PK-style door, and it belongs in the December list with a name beside it.
